Cabling Lab

Hands-on network cabling: learn the cable types and colour codes, wire and crimp an RJ45 plug, punch down a keystone jack, then test your work with a cable tester. Finish by choosing the right cable for real jobs and diagnosing faulty cables from tester results.

Task

Twisted pair: UTP and STP

An Ethernet cable holds 8 copper wires in 4 twisted pairs (orange, green, blue, brown). Each pair is twisted at a different rate. Twisting makes interference hit both wires equally, so it cancels out at the receiver, and it reduces crosstalk between pairs.

TypeShieldingUse it…
U/UTPNone (unshielded)Offices and homes. Cheap, flexible, and the most common.
F/UTP, S/FTP (STP)Foil around the cable and/or each pair, plus a drain wireNear motors, lifts, welding gear or fluorescent ballasts (high EMI). Must be earthed at the panel.

T568A and T568B pin-outs

Hold the plug with the clip facing away from you (underneath) and the gold contacts facing up, cable towards you. Pin 1 is on the left. Striped wires are white with a coloured stripe.

T568B (most common in the UK and US): W-O, O, W-G, B, W-B, G, W-Br, Br
T568A: W-G, G, W-O, B, W-B, O, W-Br, Br. It is B with the orange and green pairs swapped.

The pairs sit on pins 1-2, 3-6, 4-5 and 7-8. Pins 3 and 6 are one pair that straddles the blue pair. Keep each pair together, or you get a split pair.

Straight-through, crossover and rollover

CableWiringUsed for
Straight-throughSame standard both ends (B–B or A–A). Pin 1 → 1 … 8 → 8.Unlike devices: PC → switch, router → switch, switch → AP, patch panel → switch.
CrossoverT568A one end, T568B the other. Pins 1,2 ↔ 3,6 (Gigabit crossovers also swap 4,5 ↔ 7,8).Like devices: switch → switch, PC → PC, router → router, PC → router Ethernet port.
Rollover (console)Pin 1 → 8, 2 → 7 … 8 → 1 (pin order reversed). Usually flat light-blue, RJ45 to DB9 serial or USB.Laptop to the console port of a router or switch for setup. Terminal settings 9600 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit.
Auto-MDIX: most modern switch and NIC ports detect the cable and swap transmit/receive themselves, so a straight-through cable works for everything. Older 10/100 kit without auto-MDIX still needs a crossover between like devices, and exam questions still ask for it.

Fibre optic

Fibre carries light, not electricity. It is immune to EMI, can't be tapped easily, gives no electrical connection between buildings, and runs far beyond 100 m.

LC: small, push-and-latch, usually a duplex pair; used in SFP modules. SC: square, push-pull. ST: round bayonet, push and twist (older kit).

Coaxial cable

A single copper core inside a plastic dielectric, a braided (and often foil) shield, then the outer jacket. The shield is on the same axis as the core (co-axial), which keeps interference out.

TypeConnectorUse
RG-6 (75 Ω)F-type (screw-on)Cable broadband (DOCSIS), satellite and TV aerials
RG-59 (75 Ω)BNC (bayonet)Analogue CCTV, short video runs
RG-58 (50 Ω)BNC + T-pieceOld 10BASE2 "thinnet" Ethernet (legacy)

Power over Ethernet (PoE)

The switch or an injector (the PSE) sends DC power down the same twisted pairs as the data to a powered device (PD), so phones, access points and cameras need no mains socket. The link is still limited to 100 m.

StandardNamePower at the switch portTypical device
802.3afPoE (Type 1)15.4 WVoIP phone, basic camera
802.3atPoE+ (Type 2)30 WWi-Fi AP, PTZ camera
802.3btPoE++ (Type 3 / 4)60 W / 90 WMulti-radio APs, displays, LED lighting
Power budget: a PoE switch has a total budget (for example 370 W). Add up all the devices' needs before you plug them in.

Structured cabling

  • Patch panel: in the comms cabinet; the fixed cables from the building are punched down on the back.
  • Horizontal cable (permanent link): solid-core cable in walls and ceilings, from patch panel to wall port. Max 90 m.
  • Keystone jack / wall port: the socket in the faceplate, also punched down. It is labelled to match its patch-panel port (e.g. wall port 2.14 = panel port 14).
  • Patch leads: flexible stranded cables at each end. 10 m in total, so the whole channel is 100 m.

Tools of the trade

CrimperCuts and strips the cable, then squeezes the plug so the 8 gold contacts pierce the wires' insulation and the strain relief locks onto the jacket.
Punch-down toolPushes each wire into the IDC slot of a patch panel or keystone, then trims the waste. Keep the cut side of the blade facing outwards.
Cable testerA main unit and a remote. LEDs 1–8 light in step on both. It shows opens, shorts, reversed and crossed pairs. A qualifier also measures length and crosstalk, so it finds split pairs.
Toner & probeThe tone generator puts a warbling signal on a cable, and the probe "hears" it through the insulation. Use it to find which cable in a bundle or wall goes where, or to trace a break.

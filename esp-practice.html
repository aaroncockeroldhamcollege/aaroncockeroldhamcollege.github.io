How marks are won — and lost

Based on what separates high and low responses in the practical tasks.

Test systematically. For every requirement: a planned test, the expected result, the actual result, the action taken — and a re-test to prove the fix worked. A list of visual checks with no re-test stays in the lowest band.
Use a range of test methods. Visual inspection, the command line (ping, ipconfig), and the device console — not just “it looks right”.
Apply precise, secure configurations (see the conventions below). Addressing the vulnerabilities fully is what moves “the solution” into the top band.
Be consistent. Follow the existing naming conventions and a logical IP addressing scheme — it makes the solution maintainable and earns the organisation marks.
Reason, don't list. In design and evaluation, justify choices and weigh trade-offs (cost vs security). In the evaluation, review your own solution — not a re-run of your plan.

Secure-config conventions checklist

Think in three kinds of risk

A strong security plan covers all three — don't stop at the technical ones:

Physical locks, cable/switch access, device theft, shoulder-surfing, tailgating.
Logical / software weak encryption, open ports/services, unpatched systems, malicious QR codes/links.
User-based phishing & social engineering, unlocked devices, weak passwords, poor training.
